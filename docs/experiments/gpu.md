**Baseline disposition:** The production optical solver remains CPU-based. This page is a curated historical map; **no GPU experiments were rerun for #140.12.52**. `WebGPU` compute for optics, `WebGL2` geometry drawing and GPU adapter capability checks are three separate things. The latter two do not inherit the WebGPU optics verdict.

## Why apparent speedups are not contradictory

1. **Independent IVP batches:** many rays advance in parallel; CPU comparison measures the same fixed-step batch, including GPU upload/readback but generally excluding device/pipeline startup and scene composition. This workload can have large speedups.
2. **Target shooting:** each target needs roots, branch selection, residual convergence and *prior* first-ground-contact classification. The #140.10 hybrid repeated CPU work, leading to a measured cold regression even though standalone ray integration was fast.
3. **Whole scene:** target sampling, adaptive silhouette/foundation reconstruction, status topology, first-hit ordering, CPU compositing and Canvas painting must also match. Per-ray angular or metric errors can cross a hard contour/pixel boundary. A faster `WGSL` loop does not establish a faster or equally correct frame.
4. **Hardware evidence:** “WebGPU available” ≠ known physical dedicated hardware. Node tests/mock adapter ≠ real WGSL compilation. The committed measurements were recorded in a user browser on Intel Gen-12LP (hardware acceleration *unverified*); no claim of cross-driver portability follows.

## Physics and precision stop gates

- `f32` drift on long trajectories, even in **Vacuum**, is evidence of coordinate accumulation effects; Kahan/double-single are candidates, not automatic f64 equivalence.
- **Target tolerance** (e.g. 0.005 m) applies to endpoint residual; **first ground hit and grazing** require independent sampling/classification and event tolerance. An uncertain/blocked result may not be silently labelled visible.
- **Per-pixel comparisons** require both sides to render the *same* scene, camera and representation. In particular the #140.12.33 and #140.12.41 sampled-position maps cannot serve as full-frame diffs.
- Full acceptance needs a bounded predeclared workload, real shader compile, repeatable timings with setup and readback, CPU numerical oracle, statuses/contacts/contours and strict or clearly justified pixel agreement. Individual 48- or 128-point diagnostics cannot authorize promotion.

## Relevant source and retained instrumentation

- Canonical research shell: `tools/webgpu-optics.html`, plus `src/diagnostics/CurvatureWebGPU*.js` and GPU diagnostic helpers. Keep **one** HTML research page until planned retirement; no version-numbered copies.
- Compare diagnostics: `CurvatureModern.html`, `src/ui/modern/ModernApp.js`, `ModernCompareModel.js`, and GPU-specific diagnostic modules (some are shared with CPU paths). These are **not independently removable by filename**.
- Reproducible 12-tower target input: `tools/reference/webgpu-real-scene-targets-14010.json` generated by `tools/generate-webgpu-scene-targets-14010.js`; fixture is not a measurement.
- WebGPU tests: `tests/WebGPU*.test.js` and GPU-named tests. Read test and export consumers before removing callers; they may encode useful failure behavior as well as research plumbing.
